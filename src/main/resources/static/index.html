How it works

Four steps, and the browser is never the thing doing the signing.

  1. 1

    Send the document

    Upload a PDF and say who has to sign it. Signers who have no account are invited by e-mail with a link that works once.

  2. 2

    Each signer opens it on their own computer

    The website hands over to the SignFlow desktop application, which is where the card reader is. Nothing about the card is sent to the server.

  3. 3

    They sign with their card

    Card in, PIN on their own keyboard, signature placed where they choose. The signed revision goes back to the server, which checks it before accepting it.

  4. 4

    Everyone gets the signed PDF

    Once the last signature is in, the finished document is there to download for the sender and every signer.

How it is kept safe

Four decisions that do most of the work.

The key stays on the card

Signing happens on the signer's machine through PKCS#11. The server never sees a private key or a PIN, and there is nothing in an account that could sign on the holder's behalf.

A signature is checked, not trusted

A submitted revision is accepted only if it is an incremental update of the exact revision it claims to follow, its whole signature chain verifies, and the certificate is the one bound to that slot.

Nothing is revealed by accident

A document you are not part of answers exactly as one that does not exist. Sign-up, password reset and resend all answer the same way whether or not the address is known.

Your session is a cookie, not a token

An HttpOnly, host-locked session cookie with a CSRF token on every state-changing request. The page keeps no credential, because there is none for it to keep.
